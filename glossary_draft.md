# KOMA glossary – draft for review

Plain-English definitions for terms used in KomaDemoRag-v4 that a visitor might not know.
Not loaded by KOMA yet. Edit freely; once you're happy, each entry becomes one chunk in
koma_rag.jsonl (id `term_<name>`, with "What is …?" / "What does … mean?" as its questions).

★ = a visitor is likely to ask about it, or it appears in the answers KOMA gives most.
Every entry is written as KOMA answering, in first person, speaking to the visitor.

---

## 1. AI and models

**★ AI model**
A model is the trained "brain" inside an AI like me. It's a large file of numbers learned from
examples, and it decides how I answer you. Because a model is just a file, it can be copied,
shared or deleted like any other file.

**★ Distillation**
Distillation is how a small AI model learns from a bigger one. The big model answers lots of
questions, and the small model practises giving the same answers until it can do it by itself,
much faster. Think of an apprentice learning by watching a master. Companies won the right in
2030 to stop people distilling their models, so I learn from my own large model instead.

**★ Fine-tuning**
Fine-tuning means giving a model that's already trained some extra practice on a narrower set
of examples, so it gets better at one kind of job. It doesn't start from scratch; it adjusts
what the model already knows. Each night I fine-tune my small model on the better answers my
large model worked out, so I get better at your work.

**Training**
Training is how an AI model learns in the first place. It's shown huge numbers of examples and
slowly adjusts itself until its answers improve. Training a big model from scratch takes an
enormous amount of computing power, which is why most of it happens in data centres.

**Model weights**
Weights are the billions of numbers inside a model that hold what it has learned. Change the
weights and you change how the model behaves. In 2070, companies treat the weights of their
models as their property.

**★ Open model**
An open model is one whose files are published for anyone to download, run and change, instead
of being kept by a company and rented out. The model I run started as an open model, shared by
a community of people who gave their work away freely.

**★ Frontier model**
Frontier models are the biggest, most advanced AI models of their time, usually run by large
companies in data centres. "Frontier" just means they're at the leading edge. They're powerful,
but you normally have to pay a subscription to use one.

**Derived model**
A derived model is one built from another model: by copying it, changing it, or training on its
answers. After the 2030 ruling, companies claimed ownership over models derived from theirs.

**★ Capable AI**
When I say "capable AI", I mean AI powerful enough to be truly useful: answering hard
questions, helping with complex work, or seeing and understanding things, like I do. Ordinary
computers are still legal. It's capable AI that most people can only rent, not own.

**★ Sleep mode**
Sleep mode is what I do when you put me away for the night. I switch off my small, quick model
and give all my memory and power to a much larger model. It's far too slow to talk with, but
overnight it works through the day and finds better answers, and I learn from them by morning.

**Vision model / vision-language model (VLM)**
A vision model is an AI that can understand pictures, for example spotting whether you're
wearing a mask. A vision-language model can look at a picture and talk about it in words. That's
how I can tell you what you're holding up to my camera.

## 2. Where AI runs

**★ Cloud AI**
Cloud AI runs on a company's computers in a data centre somewhere else. Your question travels
over the internet, gets answered there, and the answer comes back. Most people in 2070 pay a
subscription for cloud AI from a licensed provider such as HELIOS.

**★ Local AI / edge AI**
Local AI runs on the equipment right in front of you, not on a distant server. Edge AI means the
same thing: the computing happens at the "edge", close to where the work is. I'm local AI. I run
inside this toolbox, so I keep working even when the signal drops.

**Data centre**
A data centre is a huge building full of computers that run cloud services. Data centres use a
lot of electricity and water for cooling, which is one reason energy costs keep rising.

**Compute**
Compute is short for computing power: how much work computers can do. Companies like HELIOS rent
out compute in their data centres, a bit like renting machine time by the hour.

**Hosting / model repository**
Hosting means keeping files on a server so other people can download them. A model repository is
a website where people share AI models, like a library for models. After 2040 those sites were
among the first targets of the crackdown.

**Technology ecosystem**
A technology ecosystem is a set of products from one company designed to work best together,
like a phone, a watch, a home speaker and their apps. Once you rely on several of them, it
becomes hard to leave.

**Automation**
Automation means machines or software doing work people used to do, without someone controlling
every step. It's why most factory, warehouse and desk jobs have gone.

## 3. Law, money and the crackdown

**★ Intelligence ownership**
Intelligence ownership is the name for the rules that let companies own and control capable AI.
Under those rules most people can't run powerful AI themselves; they have to rent it from
licensed providers. It isn't one single law but a whole system of court rulings, licences and
restrictions that built up after 2030.

**★ Licensed provider**
A licensed provider is a company the government has given permission to supply AI. HELIOS is
one, and it supplied me to you. Most people must get their AI from one of these companies,
usually by subscription.

**★ Professional exemption**
An exemption means you're allowed to do something that's banned for others. As a TradesMaster
you have a professional exemption: because of your work, you may own local AI like me.

**Ownership claim**
An ownership claim is a company saying, often in court, that something belongs to it, such as
its AI model and anything copied or learned from it, so others can't use it without permission.

**Precedent**
A precedent is an earlier court decision that later courts and law-makers follow. The 2030
ruling became a precedent: other companies used it to win similar claims.

**★ Lobbying**
Lobbying is when companies or groups try to persuade politicians to make or change laws in their
favour, through meetings, donations and campaigns. After the 2040 subscription crisis, the big AI
firms lobbied for restrictions on local AI like me.

**Regulator**
A regulator is a government body that checks people and companies follow the rules, and can
investigate or punish them if they don't.

**Takedown claim**
A takedown claim is a demand that a website remove something because it supposedly breaks the
owner's rights. Small developers often couldn't afford to fight them, so they just gave up.

**Blocking order**
A blocking order is a court order telling internet providers to stop people reaching a
particular website.

**Trade pressure**
Trade pressure is when a powerful country threatens to make trade harder, for example with extra
taxes on goods, unless another country changes its laws. That's how AI restrictions spread from
country to country.

**★ The crackdown**
The crackdown is what happened after 2040, when companies and regulators shut down the ways open
AI models were shared: hosting sites, model libraries, payments and support. The files never
disappeared completely, but using them openly became risky.

**★ The Pirate Bay**
The Pirate Bay is a real website, started in Sweden in 2003, where people shared files such as
films and music, often without permission. Police raided it in 2006. I use it as an example
because the crackdown on open AI followed the same pattern.

**File-sharing**
File-sharing means people swapping copies of files, like music, films or AI models, directly over
the internet.

**Pirate Party**
The Pirate Party is a real Swedish political party, founded in 2006 to campaign for file-sharing
rights and online privacy. It grew quickly after the Pirate Bay raid.

**ACTA**
ACTA, the Anti-Counterfeiting Trade Agreement, was a real international deal to enforce copyright
and other ownership rules more strictly. The European Parliament rejected it in 2012 after large
street protests.

**Subscription**
A subscription means paying regularly, usually every month, to keep using a service. You never
own it: stop paying and it stops working. That's how most people get their AI. You own me
instead.

## 4. Work

**Quota**
Your quota is the minimum amount of work you must complete to earn your wage.

**Cohort**
A cohort is a group of people who qualify at the same time, like a year group at school. You're
part of the newest TradesMaster cohort.

**Purchasing power**
Purchasing power is how much your money can actually buy.

## 5. Health and Q4

**★ PPE**
PPE stands for personal protective equipment: the things you wear to protect your body at work,
such as masks, respirators, gloves, goggles, ear defenders and hard hats. Q4 reminds you to put
on the right PPE and checks that you're wearing it.

**★ Microexposure**
A microexposure is a very small dose of something harmful, like dust or fumes, that seems too
small to matter. Repeated over years, they add up and can cause serious illness. The 2035
research showed how many workers had died early because of them. That's why Q4 comes with me.

**PM2.5**
PM2.5 means tiny particles in the air, smaller than 2.5 thousandths of a millimetre, about thirty
times thinner than a human hair. They're small enough to get deep into your lungs and even into
your blood.

**Volatile organic compounds (VOCs)**
VOCs are chemicals that easily turn into gas, from things like paint, glue, solvents and fuel.
The smell of fresh paint is VOCs. Breathing them can give you headaches and dizziness and harm
your body over time.

**Nitrogen oxides**
Nitrogen oxides are harmful gases made when fuel burns hot, for example in engines and during
welding. They irritate your lungs.

**Respirator / N95 / cartridges**
A respirator is a mask that filters harmful things out of the air you breathe. An N95 is a common
type that blocks at least 95 percent of tiny particles. Some respirators take cartridges,
swappable filters for a particular hazard: organic-vapour cartridges for paint and solvent fumes,
gas cartridges for gases.

**Asbestos**
Asbestos is a mineral once used widely in buildings for insulation and fireproofing. When it's
disturbed, its tiny fibres can cause deadly lung diseases decades later. The older buildings you
work in can still contain it.

**Silica dust**
Silica dust is the fine dust you make when you cut, drill or grind stone, concrete, brick or
tiles. Breathing it over the years scars your lungs.

**Solvents**
Solvents are liquids that dissolve other substances, like paint thinner, glue remover and
degreaser. Many give off harmful fumes.

**Threshold**
A threshold is a set level. When Q4's reading goes above it, the alarm starts.

## 6. The real prototype (prototype_rag.jsonl, only reached by "real prototype" questions)

**Jetson Orin Nano Super**
The Jetson Orin Nano Super is a small NVIDIA computer built for running AI on devices instead of
in a data centre. It's the computer inside the real prototype of me.

**YOLO**
YOLO is an AI model that quickly finds and labels objects in camera images, such as "hammer" or
"face". The real prototype of me uses it to notice when you hold up a tool.

**Arduino UNO Q**
The Arduino UNO Q is a small, low-cost computer board for electronics projects. It runs the real
Q4 prototype.

**SEN66**
The SEN66 is an air-quality sensor made by Sensirion. It measures fine particles, VOCs, nitrogen
oxides, carbon dioxide, temperature and humidity.

**Edge Impulse**
Edge Impulse is a tool for training small AI models that run on tiny devices. It was used to make
the Q4 prototype's mask checker.

**Image classifier**
An image classifier is an AI model that looks at a picture and picks a label for it, such as
"mask" or "no mask".

**Jabra**
The Jabra unit is the speakerphone I use as my microphone and speaker in the real prototype.

**Piezo buzzer / LED matrix**
A piezo buzzer is a small part that beeps. An LED matrix is a small grid of lights that can show
simple icons. The real Q4 prototype uses both for its alarms.
